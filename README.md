# SiteHunter: Find Startup Ideas Backed by Real Traffic Data

I've been hunting for my next side project for a while. My bookmarks folder is full of "10 SaaS ideas to build in 2026" posts, and every single one is just one blogger guessing. Then it clicked: instead of reading opinions, why not look at what the market is actually clicking on?

That's exactly what [SiteHunter](https://sitehunter.org) does. It tracks thousands of AI products every month, ranks them by real visits and growth, and shows you who's winning before anyone writes about it. Free to use, and site reports don't even need a sign-up.

Here's what kept me coming back.

## The growth leaderboard is addictive

The [Top AI Websites board](https://sitehunter.org/ai-ranking) ranks AI sites by monthly traffic, thousands of them. But the real magic is switching the sort to growth. Suddenly you're looking at the fastest rising sites this month. A product jumping from 50K to 500K visits just found its market. I check it every Monday like a newspaper.

## Size up any competitor in minutes

Type in any public domain and you get the full report: monthly visits, global rank, a multi-month trend line, traffic sources, top keywords with volume and CPC, country breakdown, and the domain's registration date. I ran it on three "competitors" I was worried about. Two were flat, one was declining. Suddenly my niche looked a lot more open than I thought.

## Micro SaaS ideas that already collect payments

My favorite board. The [Micro SaaS leaderboard](https://sitehunter.org/micro-saas-ideas) ranks small products by payment traffic, meaning every single row is a site pushing visitors into Stripe checkout. Real people paying real money. You can see who just showed up, who's surging, and who's been evergreen for years. The lesson I took from it: the best ideas are one step narrower than a proven winner.

## Backlinks where the traffic actually is

If you're launching an AI product, you'll probably submit it to a bunch of directories. Most advice stops there. SiteHunter [ranks those directories](https://sitehunter.org/ai-directories) by real monthly visits, growth, and domain age, so you skip the ghost towns and spend your time where listings actually send clicks. Submit in order, watch referral traffic show up.

## New model releases, all in one place

The [model tracker](https://sitehunter.org/new-ai-models) covers 29 labs including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek, Qwen, and Zhipu AI. Filter by provider or model type, page back through an archive that reaches 2017, and every row links to the official announcement. Completely free. If you build on top of LLMs, the release rhythm alone is worth a bookmark.

## My 30 second validation routine

Open a site, read two numbers: the registration date and the traffic curve. A new domain on a steep curve is demand validating itself in real time. An old domain still climbing is a playbook worth borrowing. One example that stuck with me: musci.io, registered in late 2025, grew from 95.8K to 597.9K monthly visits in three months. That's the shape of a validated idea.
